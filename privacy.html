Privacy

Last updated October 22, 2026

ATLAS is a free school organizer made by one person. Your work is saved in the browser you use it in. If you make an account, a copy is kept under that account so it follows you to your other devices.

What ATLAS keeps, and where

Your classes, assignments, notes, flashcards, tutor chats, grades, focus history and settings are saved in the browser you use ATLAS in.

If you make an account (with your email or with Google), a copy of your desk is also stored under that account in ATLAS's database, Google Cloud Firestore. Its security rules let only your signed-in account read or write your desk. Nobody else using ATLAS can see it.

Be aware: your desk is not end-to-end encrypted. The person who runs ATLAS administers the database and could technically open it, but doesn't look at people's desks and never shares or sells them. If that isn't good enough for something, keep it out of ATLAS, or use ATLAS without an account so nothing leaves your browser.

Your account

  • Email accounts: your email address, an optional first name, and your password. Passwords are handled by Google's Firebase Authentication and stored there in hashed form. ATLAS never sees or keeps your password.
  • Google accounts: your name, email address and profile picture, used only to show who is signed in.
  • ATLAS emails you only to confirm your address or to reset your password. Those emails come from Google's Firebase on ATLAS's behalf.

ATLAS does not read your email, calendar, contacts or files. Its use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

An older ATLAS kept desks in a hidden folder in your Google Drive. If you choose to bring that copy in, ATLAS reads only that hidden folder, once, when you ask.

Reminders

Reminders are off until you turn them on, and each device asks for its own permission.

  • While ATLAS is open, your device shows reminders itself.
  • To get them when ATLAS is closed, the next two weeks of reminders are kept under your account, with the push address your browser gives that device. That means the time, plus the assignment's name unless you turn off "Show what's due". A small script on Google's servers, run by the person who runs ATLAS, reads only that list and sends each reminder when it's due through Firebase Cloud Messaging. It never opens your desk.
  • Turning reminders off, signing out or deleting your account removes the list and the push address.

Google Classroom

ATLAS never signs in to Google Classroom and never asks for permission to see it. When you use "Send to ATLAS" or paste your Classroom page, ATLAS reads the text and links of that one page, in your browser, to find your classes and what's due. What it reads goes from the Classroom tab straight to ATLAS in your browser, inside the web address after a # sign, which browsers never send to any server. Only the classes and assignments you choose to add are kept, with the rest of your desk.

Moving your desk to another account

To move your desk to an account with a different email, you sign in to both. ATLAS saves your desk to the old account, signs out, and once you've signed in to the new one, copies the desk from your device straight into it. Nobody else is involved. The old account keeps its copy until you delete it.

Device lock

If you set a passkey or a pattern to lock ATLAS on a device, it stays on that device. A passkey is checked by your device itself (Face ID, a fingerprint, Windows Hello or the device PIN). ATLAS stores only a scrambled form of a pattern, never the pattern itself. Neither is sent anywhere.

AI study tools and the tutor

The AI features (the tutor, summarize, make flashcards, quiz me and the rest) only run when you use them. When they do, what you ask, and the page you point them at, is sent to Google:

  • If your computer has Chrome's on-device AI, some of it can run on the computer itself, and nothing leaves it.
  • Otherwise it goes to Google's Gemini service through ATLAS's Firebase project. Google's free tier is used, and Google may use what you send to improve its products. Please leave anything private out.

Agent mode lets the AI make changes on your desk for you. When you send it a request, ATLAS also sends a summary of your desk so it knows what's there: your classes and their times, what's due and the start of its notes, your deck and page titles, your grades, your upcoming calendar and your reminders. It doesn't send the text of your pages or cards unless it needs one for what you asked. The changes happen in your browser, and every one is listed with an Undo.

Your tutor chats are saved with your desk, like everything else. To stop other sites from using ATLAS's free AI, it's protected by reCAPTCHA; Google's Privacy Policy and Terms of Service apply. You can switch the AI tools off in Settings.

What ATLAS never does

  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and nothing is sold or shared.
  • ATLAS loads its typefaces from Google Fonts, which sees your IP address like any website you visit.

Deleting your data

  • Your account: in ATLAS, open You (your name in the corner), then Delete account. This removes the account, its desk and its reminders from ATLAS's cloud straight away.
  • This browser: open Settings, then Your data, then Erase everything.
  • An old Google Drive copy: in Google Drive, open Settings, then Manage apps, find ATLAS and choose Delete hidden app data. To take back ATLAS's access to your Google account, visit myaccount.google.com/connections.

Students

ATLAS is made for students. If you are under the age where your country lets you agree to this on your own, use ATLAS with a parent's or guardian's permission. School-issued Google accounts may need the school's approval before they can sign in. You can always make an ATLAS account with your email instead, or use ATLAS without an account.
